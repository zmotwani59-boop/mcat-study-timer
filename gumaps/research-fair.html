Start a conversation

You don't have to know everything about a field before approaching a lab. Ask about the work, listen, and follow up when something interests you.

What to ask

  • What problem is your team working on right now?
  • What would an undergraduate actually do in your group?
  • Is previous research experience required?
  • What time commitment do you typically expect?
  • What's the best way to apply or stay in touch?

Following up

  1. Write down whom you met and the part of the research that interested you.
  2. Use the application or contact link in the corresponding group listing.
  3. Send a short personalized follow-up within a few days.
  4. Be specific about your interests and ask about next steps without assuming an opening.

A simple follow-up email

Keep it brief and personalize the highlighted parts. If the group asks for an application, complete that instead of substituting an email.

Subject: Great meeting you at the GUMAPS Research Fair

Dear [Name],

Thank you for speaking with me at the GUMAPS Research Fair. I enjoyed learning about [specific project or research question], particularly [what interested you].

I'm a Georgetown undergraduate studying [major/year], and I'm interested in getting involved in this area. Could you let me know whether there are opportunities for undergraduate involvement and the best next step?

Thank you again for your time.

Best,
[Your name]
